# Pages 29–36 — Merchandise & Short-Form Content System

## Strategic Principle

Merchandise and content must reinforce the same emotional codes.

A successful SINDAENG product should not feel like a logo placed on an object. It should translate a specific emotional behavior into a useful, collectible, or shareable form.

---

## Page 29 — Merchandise Architecture

### Tier 1: Entry Goods

- Stickers
- Postcards
- Acrylic charms
- Badges
- Phone wallpapers
- Digital stickers

Purpose: affordable discovery and emotional self-expression.

### Tier 2: Lifestyle Goods

- Pouches
- Tote bags
- Mugs
- Apparel
- Phone cases
- Desk accessories

Purpose: daily visibility and brand familiarity.

### Tier 3: Emotional Objects

- Plush
- Night lights
- Cushions
- Scent objects
- Small music boxes

Purpose: translate the IP into comfort, atmosphere, and companionship.

### Tier 4: Collector Goods

- Vinyl figures
- Resin editions
- Dioramas
- Numbered prints
- Exhibition exclusives

Purpose: scarcity, display value, and long-term brand equity.

## Page 30 — Product-by-Series Matrix

| Series | Strongest goods | Emotional use |
|---|---|---|
| Bloom | Stationery, apparel, spring accessories | Optimism and gifting |
| Moss | Plush, cushions, sleep goods, lamps | Rest and healing |
| Goblin | Keyrings, blind boxes, streetwear | Mischief and confidence |
| Spirit | Mobile goods, animation assets, translucent figures | Imagination and companionship |
| Weather | Umbrellas, mugs, seasonal stationery | Mood and daily routine |
| Cosmos | Premium figures, lighting, metallic accessories | Dream and collectability |

### Rule

Not every design belongs on every product. Product selection should reflect the emotional function of the series.

---

## Page 31 — Packaging System

### Required Elements

- SINDAENG wordmark
- Bloom Code™
- Series name
- Emotional keyword
- Small narrative sentence
- Authenticity or edition information

### Visual Hierarchy

1. Character image
2. Series and emotional keyword
3. Product information
4. Legal and manufacturing information

### Sustainable Direction

- Reduce oversized plastic windows
- Use recyclable paper structures where practical
- Design box interiors as collectible backgrounds
- Avoid packaging volume that exceeds the product experience

---

## Page 32 — Short-Form Content Formula

### Recommended Duration

5–15 seconds for core episodes.

### Narrative Pattern

1. Ordinary situation
2. Small emotional trigger
3. Emotion Bloom™ reacts
4. Unexpected visual payoff
5. Return to SINDAENG’s calm baseline

### Example

- SINDAENG waits for a bus.
- One raindrop falls.
- A tiny cloud grows above the head.
- The cloud opens a miniature umbrella for itself.
- SINDAENG remains expressionless and walks away.

### Key Benefit

The story works without dialogue and can travel across languages.

---

## Page 33 — Content Pillars

### 1. Everyday Mood

Eating, waiting, walking, sleeping, working, missing the bus.

### 2. Emotion Bloom™ Discovery

A new emotional object appears and reveals its behavior.

### 3. Korean Everyday Fantasy

Markets, seasons, rooftops, alleys, snacks, textiles, weather, and rituals interpreted through the IP world.

### 4. Art-Toy Stories

Figures come alive, escape packaging, or interact with collectors’ spaces.

### 5. Making SINDAENG

Sculpting, animation tests, color development, packaging, and prototype stories.

---

## Page 34 — Publishing Rhythm

### Initial 12-Week Test

- 3 short videos per week
- 1 recurring format per weekday
- Reuse platform-safe master files for Shorts, Reels, and TikTok

### Suggested Weekly Pattern

- Monday: Everyday Mood
- Wednesday: Emotion Bloom™ reveal
- Friday: Visual joke or mini-adventure

### Data to Track

- Three-second hold rate
- Completion rate
- Rewatch rate
- Saves and shares
- Comments naming the emotion or favorite Bloom
- Profile visits and follower conversion

### Learning Goal

Determine which emotional codes create recognition, not merely which videos receive accidental views.

---

## Page 35 — Content-to-Product Loop

```text
Short-form test
→ audience reaction
→ winning Bloom Code™
→ prototype
→ preorder or limited release
→ collector response
→ expanded story and merchandise
```

### Example

If a Moss episode receives high saves and comments such as “this is me,” prioritize calming lifestyle goods before producing a large vinyl run.

### Principle

Content is not only promotion. It is low-cost product research.

---

## Page 36 — Brand Safety for Content

### Preserve

- Calm facial baseline
- Clear silhouette
- Emotional object logic
- Short, visually readable stories
- Warm humor without cruelty

### Avoid

- Trend-chasing that breaks character identity
- Excessive meme language that ages quickly
- Graphic fear, humiliation, or aggressive slapstick
- Random costume changes without emotional purpose
- Product placement that overwhelms the story

### Final Test

A viewer should understand the mood even with sound off, subtitles off, and the logo removed.
